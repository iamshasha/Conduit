Conduit

Reach past
the browser.

Conduit is an app which allows websites to access users' folders, save onto the local device into their designated sandbox and read hardware information directly, while still giving the user maximum controls over the permissions.

Click anywhere. Every click sends a signal down the channel.

How it works

Four things to know

It lives quietly in the background

After install, Conduit runs as a tray icon on Windows and Linux, or a menu-bar item on macOS. It listens only on your own machine, at 127.0.0.1. Nothing is exposed to the internet.

A site has to ask, and you answer

The first time a page wants access, a prompt appears. Deny is selected by default, and Allow only becomes clickable after a moment, so a stray click or a held key can't approve anything. You pick exactly which permissions to grant.

You stay in control from the dashboard

Open Conduit any time to see every site you've approved, change or revoke its permissions, browse the files it has saved, and read a log of what it has done. One click takes any of it away.

Connect a project

Building in Scratch or TurboWarp? Load the Conduit extension and your project can save large files, read system stats, and more, each still gated by your consent. The extension URL is on the dashboard's Extension page.

What a site can ask for

Permissions, one at a time

Storage

Keep real files in a per-site folder with far more room than cookies or local storage, wiped when you revoke the site.

Launch apps

Open an application by path. You approve each program the first time; Conduit can remember the ones you trust.

Hardware & stats

Read CPU, memory, GPU, battery, and network figures, the same numbers your system monitor shows.

Media, power & clipboard

Adjust volume, control playback, read the clipboard, or sleep the machine, each behind its own prompt.

Safe and Fast

It runs locally on your device unexposed to the internet, so nothing on the internet can access it. It's also designed to be extremely efficient and small. It uses very little memory when idle and sits at 0% CPU usage.

Your device, you decide.

You can control how much permission you give to each website easily within our dashboard, and even revoke all permissions instantly. When the website asks for permission, you can always choose to allow or reject it.

Designs and Choices

We chose to use the native UI (WinUI 3 for Windows, GTK for Linux and Swift for Mac) instead of wrapping websites because we hope the app can provide maximum efficiency and the smallest package size on all platforms.
